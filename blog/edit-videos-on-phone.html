How to Edit Videos on Your Phone: A Beginner Guide

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

You do not need a computer to edit good videos. A modern phone and a free app are enough to cut clips, clean up sound, add text and export for YouTube, Reels or Shorts. This guide walks you through the basic steps in the order that most editors follow them.

1. Plan before you edit

Decide what the video is about and how long it should be. Write a short outline, even if it is only a few lines. Editing is much faster when you know what you want to keep. Delete bad takes on your phone before you start so you do not waste time scrolling.

2. Choose a free editing app

Popular free choices include CapCut, and many phones also come with a built-in editor. Free features and limits can change, so check what each app offers before you pick one. Choose an app that feels simple to you, because you will use it often. For more options, see our list of free tools for creators.

3. Import and organise your clips

Create a new project and add your clips in the order of your outline. Keep your main footage on the main track. If you have extra shots to cover cuts, such as close-ups or cutaway shots, add them after you finish the first rough edit.

4. Trim and cut

Remove the empty start and end of each clip, and cut out pauses, mistakes and repeated words. Use the split tool to cut in the middle of a clip. Tight cuts keep viewers interested. A good rule is to cut anything that does not move the story or the message forward.

5. Fix the sound first

Viewers forgive average video much faster than bad sound. Lower the volume of music so that the voice is always clear. If your app has a noise reduction option, try it, and check the result with headphones. Keep the volume similar from clip to clip.

6. Add music and sound effects carefully

Use music that you have the right to use. Many apps and platforms have libraries of music for creators, but the rules for each track can differ, so read the licence. Copyrighted music can lead to claims or muted videos. Keep the music softer than the speech.

7. Add text and captions

Many people watch with the sound off, so on-screen text and captions help a lot. Use a big, readable font and keep the text inside the safe area of the screen. If your app creates automatic captions, read them and fix mistakes, especially names and local words.

8. Use transitions and effects sparingly

A simple cut works best most of the time. Too many flashy transitions look distracting and can feel amateur. Use effects to support the story, such as a quick zoom to highlight a moment, and not just because the app offers them.

9. Adjust colour and brightness

Make small changes to brightness, contrast and colour so your clips look similar to each other. If you filmed in different light, try to match them. For a short film, a consistent colour look can make it feel more professional. Do not push the sliders too far.

10. Choose the right shape and export settings

Use a vertical 9:16 shape for Reels and Shorts, and a widescreen 16:9 shape for regular YouTube videos. Export at 1080p if your phone and app allow it. Higher quality makes bigger files, so make sure you have enough storage and a good internet connection to upload.

11. Watch the final video before posting

Play the exported video from start to finish on your phone, ideally with headphones. Check the sound, the text and the ending. It is much easier to fix a mistake before you upload than after. Then add a clear title, description and thumbnail. Our guide to writing descriptions can help with the next step.

12. Save your project and keep improving

Keep your project files so you can make changes later. Watch videos from creators you like and notice how they cut and pace their scenes. Every edit makes you faster. If you are working on a story, our guide to writing a short film script can help you plan what to shoot.
