10 Free AI Tools Every Creator Should Know

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

Making videos takes time, and a lot of that time goes into small jobs like writing titles, designing thumbnails, cleaning audio and adding captions. Free tools can take a big part of that work off your hands. Here are ten kinds of tools that creators use every day, with examples. One important note: free plans, limits and features change often, so always check each tool's own website for the latest details.

1. AI chat assistants for ideas and scripts

Assistants such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini have free plans that can help you brainstorm video ideas, outline a script or rewrite a rough paragraph. Treat the answer as a first draft. Rewrite it in your own voice and double check any facts before you use them.

2. Viralora for titles, captions and hooks

This site has free tools that help you write YouTube titles, Instagram captions, hashtags and opening lines. They need no sign-up. Generate a few options, then edit the best one so it sounds like you.

3. Canva for thumbnails and posters

Canva's free plan has ready templates for thumbnails, Reel covers and posts. Pick a simple layout with large readable text and strong contrast. Keep the same style across your videos so people recognise your channel.

4. CapCut for editing on your phone

CapCut is popular for quick edits on a phone. It offers cuts, transitions, text, templates and automatic captions. It is a good place to start if you do not have a computer for editing.

5. DaVinci Resolve for editing on a computer

DaVinci Resolve has a free version with professional editing and colour tools. It needs a reasonably good computer and some time to learn, but it is a strong choice for short films and longer videos.

6. Audacity for cleaning audio

Bad sound makes viewers leave faster than bad video. Audacity is a free, open source audio editor that can reduce background noise and balance volume. Even a few minutes of cleanup can make a big difference.

7. Text to speech tools for voiceovers

Some services, such as ElevenLabs, offer limited free plans that turn text into speech. They can help when you cannot record your own voice. Check the licence and usage rules before you use the audio in public videos, and be honest with viewers if the voice is synthetic.

8. Background remover tools

Tools like remove.bg, or the background remover inside Canva, cut a person or object out of a photo. This makes thumbnails cleaner and helps your subject stand out.

9. Automatic captions

Many people watch with the sound off. YouTube Studio and CapCut can create captions automatically. Always read them and fix mistakes, especially names and local words, because automatic captions are not perfect.

10. YouTube Studio and Instagram Insights

These free built-in tools may be the most useful of all. They show which videos bring new viewers, where people stop watching, and which Reels get saves and shares. Check them every week and let the numbers guide your next idea.

How to use AI tools well

AI is an assistant, not a replacement for your own ideas. Do not publish raw output without reading it. Do not copy other people's work, and respect the licence of any image, music or voice you use. The creators who gain the most are the ones who use tools to save time and then spend that time on better stories.
