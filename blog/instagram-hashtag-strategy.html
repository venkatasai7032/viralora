Instagram Hashtag Strategy for Creators: A Simple Guide

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

Hashtags help Instagram understand what your post is about and help people interested in that topic find it. They are not magic, and a long list of random tags will not make a weak post go viral. Used carefully, though, a small set of relevant hashtags can support your content. This guide explains how to choose them and how to check whether they work.

1. Use fewer, more relevant hashtags

You do not need to fill every post with tags. Around three to five hashtags that truly describe your post is a good starting point. Choose tags that match the subject, the format and the audience. If a hashtag does not describe your post, leave it out.

2. Mix broad and niche hashtags

Very popular hashtags have millions of posts, so your post can disappear quickly. Very small ones have too few viewers to matter. A healthy mix works best: one or two broad tags for the main topic, and two or three more specific ones for your niche or community. For a filmmaker, a broad tag might be about filmmaking, and a niche tag might be about short films in your own language.

3. Research before you choose

Type a keyword into the Instagram search bar and look at the hashtag suggestions. Open a hashtag and look at the top posts. If they are similar to your content, the tag is a good fit. If they are about something else, skip it.

4. Learn from creators like you

Look at accounts in your niche that are a little ahead of you. Notice which hashtags they use on their best posts. Do not copy their whole list. Use it as a source of ideas and then test the tags on your own account.

5. Do not use the same set every time

Pasting the same block of tags under every post looks lazy and does not describe each post well. Keep a small bank of tags for each of your topics and choose a fresh set that fits each post.

6. Avoid spammy or banned hashtags

Some hashtags get restricted because they are linked to spam or content that breaks the rules. Using them can hurt your reach. If a hashtag shows a warning message when you search for it, do not use it. Also avoid hashtags that have nothing to do with your post just because they are popular.

7. Where to put them

You can put hashtags at the end of the caption or in the first comment. Either way works, so pick one and stay consistent. Keep the caption itself clear and useful, because the words in the caption and on the screen also help people and Instagram understand your post.

8. Make a branded hashtag

A short, unique hashtag for your own content, such as your channel or series name, helps people find all your posts in one place. Use it on every post, along with your topic tags.

9. Check your results

Open the Insights for each post and look at where the reach came from. Compare posts with different sets of hashtags. Keep the tags that go with your better posts, and drop the ones that never seem to help. Remember that the quality of the video matters more than the tags.
