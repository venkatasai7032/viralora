How to Start a YouTube Channel from Scratch

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

Starting a YouTube channel is free, and you can begin with just a phone. The hard part is not the setup, which takes about ten minutes. The hard part is knowing what to make and staying consistent. This guide walks through the whole process in order, from your first idea to your first upload.

1. Choose a topic you can talk about for a long time

Pick something you enjoy and know enough about to make many videos. It could be short films, cooking, gaming, farming, study tips or local travel. A topic you love makes it easier to keep going when views are slow at the start.

2. Decide who your videos are for

Imagine one person who would enjoy your videos, and think about what problem you solve for them or how you entertain them. Clear answers make it easier to plan videos, write titles and design thumbnails.

3. Create your channel

Sign in to YouTube with your Google account, click your profile picture, and choose to create a channel. You can use your own name or a separate channel name. Choose a name that is short, easy to spell and fits your topic.

4. Add a profile picture, banner and description

Use a clear profile picture and a simple banner. In your channel description, explain in two or three sentences what you make and how often you upload. This helps new visitors decide whether to subscribe.

5. Start with the equipment you already have

A modern phone is enough. Good audio matters more than a fancy camera, so record in a quiet place and keep the microphone close. Film near a window for soft natural light. You can upgrade later when the channel grows.

6. Plan your first three videos

Do not wait for the perfect first video. Plan three simple ideas so you do not run out after one upload. Search your topic on YouTube and notice which questions people ask. Those questions are easy video ideas.

7. Record, edit and keep it tight

Start with the most interesting part, not a long introduction. Cut pauses, mistakes and repeated points. Apps like CapCut on a phone or DaVinci Resolve on a computer are free to start with. Add clear captions if you can.

8. Make a clear title and thumbnail

Your title should say what the viewer will get, in plain words. The thumbnail should have large text, a clear face or object and good contrast. Both should be honest about what the video contains.

9. Upload and fill in the details

When you upload, write a short description that explains the video in the first two lines. Choose the right audience setting, add a few relevant tags or hashtags, and select a thumbnail. Add the video to a playlist so viewers can keep watching your content.

10. Follow the rules

Use only music, clips and images that you have permission to use. Copied content can lead to copyright claims or strikes. Read YouTube's community guidelines so you know what is allowed.

11. Be consistent and study your analytics

Choose an upload schedule that you can keep, even if it is only one video every week or two. After a few videos, open YouTube Studio and see which videos get views and where people stop watching. Make more of what works.

12. Think about monetisation later

Earning money from YouTube has requirements set by YouTube, and they can change. Check the official YouTube Partner Program page for the current rules instead of relying on old videos or posts. At the start, focus on making good videos and building an audience.
