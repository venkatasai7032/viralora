How to Write a Short Film Script: A Beginner Guide

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

A short film script is a small story told in a few pages. Because you have very little time, every scene has to matter. Many beginners try to fit a feature film into ten minutes and end up with something rushed. This guide shows a simple way to write a short script that you can actually shoot.

1. Start with one simple idea

A short film works best with a single situation and a single main character who wants something. A man needs to deliver a letter before sunrise. A girl is afraid to tell her father about her results. Keep the idea small enough to tell in five to ten minutes.

2. Write a one sentence logline

A logline says who the story is about, what they want, and what stands in their way. Example: "A young auto driver must return a lost bag before the owner leaves town, but the only clue he has is a torn ticket." If you cannot write your idea in one or two sentences, it is probably too big or too unclear.

3. Use a simple three part structure

Setup: show the character and what they want. Conflict: put obstacles in the way, with the pressure rising. Resolution: show how it ends and how the character has changed. In a short film, the ending often comes quickly after the biggest problem, so do not stretch the middle.

4. Limit locations and characters

Every extra location and actor makes the film harder and more costly to make. Aim for one to three locations and a few characters. A well chosen single location, such as a bus stop, a kitchen or a rooftop, can feel rich if the story is strong.

5. Learn the basic script format

A screenplay has scene headings, action lines and dialogue. Action is written in the present tense and only describes what we can see or hear. Here is a small example:

INT. SMALL TEA SHOP - EARLY MORNING

RAVI (22) wipes a table slowly. His phone buzzes. He
looks at it, then turns it face down.

OWNER
(without looking up)
Answer it.

RAVI
It can wait.

One page of script is roughly one minute of screen time, so a ten page script makes a film of about ten minutes.

6. Show, do not explain

Film is a visual medium. Instead of a character saying "I am very sad", show it with an action: an untouched meal, a photo turned over, a long look out of a window. Use dialogue to add something new, not to repeat what we can already see.

7. Make the dialogue sound real

Write the way your characters would actually speak, in the language and style of their world. Read each line aloud. If it feels stiff, shorten it. People often speak in half sentences and avoid saying exactly what they feel, and that is where good dialogue gets its power.

8. Rewrite, then rewrite again

The first draft is only the start. Put it away for a day, then read it again and cut anything that does not move the story or reveal the character. Ask a friend to read it aloud and notice where they get confused or bored.

9. Think about how you will shoot it

As you write, ask whether you can film each scene with the people, places and time you have. A script that fits your real resources is better than a big script that you can never finish. When the script is ready, break it into shots and make a simple shot list.
