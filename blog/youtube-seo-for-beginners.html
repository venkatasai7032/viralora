YouTube SEO for Beginners: A Simple 6-Step Guide

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

YouTube is one of the biggest search engines in the world, and most beginners ignore search completely. You do not need expensive tools to be found. You need to help YouTube understand what your video is about and who should watch it. This guide explains the basics in simple steps that you can use on your very next video.

1. Start with keyword research

Type your topic into the YouTube search bar and read the autocomplete suggestions. These are real searches that real people make. Pick a phrase with a clear purpose, such as "how to edit videos on phone", instead of a broad word like "editing". Then search that phrase and watch the top results. If every result is from a huge channel, try a more specific version of your idea.

2. Understand what the viewer wants

Every search has a reason behind it. Someone who types "short film script format" wants to learn a format, while someone who types "best short films" wants to watch. Make a video that gives the viewer what they came for, quickly. A video that matches the search reason keeps people watching, and that tells YouTube it is a good result.

3. Optimise your title and description

Use the main keyword near the start of the title, and make it clear and honest. Write the first two lines of the description as a natural summary that also contains the keyword. Add a few paragraphs about the video, plus helpful links and timestamps. Avoid repeating the same word again and again. For more detail, read our guides on writing YouTube titles and writing video descriptions.

4. Use tags and hashtags lightly

Tags matter far less than they used to. Add a handful that describe the topic and common spelling mistakes, and use two or three relevant hashtags. The time you save here is better spent on the title, the thumbnail and the first seconds of the video.

5. Hook viewers in the first 15 seconds

Watch time and audience retention are strong signals of quality. Open with the result or the biggest promise of the video, not a long introduction. Cut pauses and repeated points in editing. If viewers leave in the first seconds, YouTube will show your video to fewer people, no matter how good your keywords are.

6. Make the thumbnail work with the title

The thumbnail and title should tell one story together. Use large readable text, a clear face or object, and high contrast. Do not repeat the exact title words on the image. See our guide on making thumbnails that get clicks for practical tips.

7. Add chapters with timestamps

Timestamps in the description split your video into chapters. They help viewers jump to the part they need, and they can also show as sections in search results. Start with 0:00 and write short, clear labels.

8. Think about language and your audience

Write your title and description in the language your viewers actually search in. If your audience searches in English, use English keywords. If they search in their own language, use that. Some creators write the title in one language and add the other language in the description. Test what works for your audience.

9. Use playlists and end screens

Group related videos into playlists so that viewers keep watching one video after another. Use end screens to point to your next video. More time spent on your channel is a good sign for YouTube and is good for your viewers too.

10. Study your analytics

In YouTube Studio, look at which videos bring new viewers, which search terms people used to find you, and where viewers stop watching. Make more of what works. If a video gets impressions but few clicks, improve the title or thumbnail. If people click but leave quickly, improve the opening.

11. Be consistent and patient

SEO does not work overnight. Upload on a schedule you can keep, improve a little with each video, and give your older videos time to be found. Our guide on getting your first 1000 subscribers covers the long-term plan.
