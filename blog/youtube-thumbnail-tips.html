How to Make YouTube Thumbnails That Get Clicks

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

A thumbnail is the small picture that people see before they decide to watch your video. Together with the title, it decides whether your video gets a click. You do not need to be a designer. You need a few clear rules, a free design tool and a bit of practice. This guide covers the basics.

1. Know what a thumbnail is for

A thumbnail has one job: to make the right viewer curious enough to click. It does not need to explain the whole video. It needs to show one clear idea at a glance, in the second or two that someone looks at it while scrolling.

2. Use the right size

Design your thumbnail at 1280 by 720 pixels, in the widescreen 16:9 shape. This makes it look sharp on phones, computers and TVs. YouTube also has a file size limit for thumbnails, so check the YouTube Help page for the current rule if your upload is rejected.

3. Show one idea only

A crowded thumbnail with many objects and lots of text is hard to understand. Choose one subject, one emotion or one result, and make it the clear focus. If you need to explain two things, you probably need two videos.

4. Keep the text short and large

Use three to five words at most. Make the letters big, thick and easy to read, even on a small phone screen. Pick a text colour that stands out strongly from the background. Do not repeat the full title. Add something the title does not say, such as a number or a short reaction word.

5. Use faces and emotion

Thumbnails with a clear human face often feel more personal, and an honest expression such as surprise, worry or joy can draw attention. Make sure the face is large, well lit and not covered by text. If your video is not about a person, use a clear close-up of the main object instead.

6. Use contrast and a simple colour palette

Bright subjects on darker backgrounds, or the other way around, stand out in a busy feed. Limit yourself to two or three main colours. Check that your thumbnail looks clear next to other videos in your topic, because it has to compete with them.

7. Make the thumbnail and title a team

If the thumbnail shows the result, let the title explain how you got there. If the title asks a question, let the thumbnail hint at the answer without giving everything away. For ideas on titles, read our guide to writing YouTube titles that get clicks.

8. Be honest

A thumbnail that shows something that is not in the video may get a click, but viewers will leave quickly and may not return. This hurts watch time and trust. Make a thumbnail that is exciting and true.

9. Check it on a phone

Most people see your thumbnail on a small screen. Shrink your design to a tiny size on your computer, or send it to your phone. If you cannot read the text or understand the picture at that size, simplify it.

10. Keep a consistent style

Using similar fonts, colours and layouts across your videos helps people recognise your channel. Do not copy another creator exactly. Choose your own simple style and use it again and again.

11. Use free tools

Canva has a free plan with thumbnail templates, and many phone editors can add text to a photo. You can also use a background remover to cut out your subject. See our list of free tools for creators for more options. Only use images, fonts and graphics that you have the right to use.

12. Test and compare

In YouTube Studio, look at impressions and click-through rate for each video. If a video gets many impressions but few clicks, try a new thumbnail and compare the results over the next week. Some channels also have a built-in feature to test thumbnails, so check Studio to see if it is available to you.
