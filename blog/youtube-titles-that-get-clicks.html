How to Write YouTube Titles That Get Clicks

By Viralora · Updated October 2026

Your title is one of the first things a viewer judges, often together with your thumbnail. A good title does not trick people. It tells them clearly what they will get and gives them a reason to click now. If your video gets many impressions but few clicks, the title is one of the first things to look at. Here is a practical way to write better ones.

1. Promise one clear outcome

Viewers click when they know exactly what they will get. Compare "My Budget" with "How I Saved 50,000 Rupees in 3 Months". The second names a result and a timeframe. Before you write a title, ask yourself: what will the viewer know or be able to do after watching this video? Put that answer into the title.

2. Create curiosity without lying

Curiosity works when the video really answers the question you raise. A phrase like "the mistake most beginners make" is fine only if you reveal that mistake. Misleading titles may bring clicks for a short time, but viewers leave quickly, watch time drops, and YouTube then shows the video to fewer people. An honest title that gets slightly fewer clicks is better than a false one that loses viewers.

3. Put the main keyword early

People find videos by searching with specific words. Place your main keyword in the first few words, for example "YouTube SEO for Beginners: 6 Simple Steps". Titles can be cut off on phones after around 60 characters, so keep the most important part at the start. If you want to learn more about keywords, read our YouTube SEO guide for beginners.

4. Use numbers and specifics

  • "7 Editing Tricks" feels more concrete than "Editing Tricks".
  • Real details such as time, cost or number of steps make a title feel believable.
  • Use true numbers from your own experience, not made-up ones.

5. Keep it short and easy to read

A long title with many ideas confuses people. Aim for one idea in a short sentence, and cut words that add nothing. Read the title aloud. If you run out of breath, it is probably too long.

6. Choose strong, simple words

Plain words usually beat fancy ones. Words that show a benefit, such as "simple", "fast", "free" or "beginner", tell the viewer the video is for them. Avoid writing the whole title in capital letters or using too many exclamation marks, because that looks like spam and hurts trust.

7. Make the title and thumbnail work together

The title and the thumbnail should tell one story. If the thumbnail shows the result, the title can explain how you got it. Do not repeat the same words in both. Add what the picture cannot show, such as the time it took or the number of steps.

8. Try these simple formulas

  • How to + result: "How to Edit a Short Film on Your Phone".
  • Number + topic: "5 Mistakes in Your First Short Film".
  • Result + time: "I Made a Short Film in 7 Days" (only if it is true).
  • Question: "Is CapCut Good Enough for Short Films?".

9. Write many versions, then choose

For every video, write at least five versions of the title. Cut the weak ones, and keep the one that is clearest and most honest. Then read it as if you were a stranger who has never seen your channel. Would you know what the video is about in two seconds?

10. Test and learn from your data

After a week, open YouTube Studio and look at impressions and click-through rate for the video. If a video gets many impressions but few clicks, change the title or thumbnail and compare the results over the next week. Change only one thing at a time so you know what caused the difference. Over time, you will see which kinds of titles work best for your audience.
